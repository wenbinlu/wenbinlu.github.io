## The question

A massive black hole can tear a stellar binary apart, ejecting one star at high speed and capturing the other. But what happens during the many passages before this breakup?

## What's found

We model repeated black-hole perturbations together with tidal interactions between the two stars. The perturbations can drive the binary to highly eccentric orbits, bringing the stars within a few stellar radii of each other. Their oscillations then grow chaotically, drawing energy from the orbit and rapidly shrinking the binary.

In the modeled regime where binaries approach the breakup boundary gradually, up to half of initially wide binaries become close binaries instead of being disrupted while still wide. Their separations can shrink to less than about ten stellar radii.

## Why it matters

This mechanism creates close binaries in galactic nuclei and provides a channel for producing the fastest hypervelocity stars when the tightened binaries eventually break apart. It also connects binary evolution near massive black holes to repeating partial tidal disruptions and quasi-periodic eruptions. The fraction that undergoes this process depends on the initial binary properties and orbital environment.
