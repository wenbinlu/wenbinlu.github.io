## What's found

We model a super-Eddington disk wind together with irradiation of the outer disk. Fits to Hubble photometry allow accretors from a $1.4M_\odot$ neutron star to a $\sim100M_\odot$ black hole. The wind’s color temperature depends weakly on mass, making these models difficult to distinguish in the UV/optical.

## Why it matters

The plateau alone does not establish an unusually massive black hole. Infrared free-free emission and recombination lines offer tests of accretor mass and wind composition. The inferred disk properties are consistent with disruption of or merger with a companion star.

Earlier in [Tsuna & Lu (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...986...84T/abstract), we propose a micro-tidal disruption event following a stripped-envelope supernova: a natal kick sends the newborn neutron star or black hole into its companion, disrupting it. Accretion of the stellar debris drives a powerful wind that collides with the supernova ejecta, providing a possible engine for LFBOTs.

For a broader introduction, our review [Ho & Lu (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260823715H/abstract) summarizes LFBOT observations, proposed origins, and promising ways to distinguish the models.
