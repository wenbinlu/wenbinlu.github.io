## The question

Can we identify neutron star mergers promptly when their narrow gamma-ray burst jets point away from us?

## What's found

We simulate a short gamma-ray burst jet propagating through merger ejecta and follow the surrounding cocoon to the radius where photons diffuse out. Its cooling produces soft X-ray flashes lasting a few to ten seconds for off-axis observers. In our canonical model, Einstein Probe could detect a GW170817-like event at viewing angles up to 45 degrees, and events with smaller viewing angles of 10 degrees can be detected  up to a distance of 1 Gpc.

## Why it matters

These flashes could trigger rapid follow-up even without a detectable gamma-ray burst. Subsequent kilonova and jet-afterglow observations would test the merger interpretation. This is a predicted counterpart whose detectability depends on the jet power, ejecta density profile, and viewing angle.

This work extends our studies of jet-driven cocoons in stellar explosions. In [Zheng & Lu (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1003L..19Z/abstract), we show that cooling cocoons from long gamma-ray burst jets viewed off axis can produce fast X-ray transients without detectable gamma-ray counterparts. In [Zheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...985...21Z/abstract), we model EP240414a as an off-axis jet–cocoon system, connecting its X-ray flash, optical emission, and radio afterglow to a stellar explosion.
