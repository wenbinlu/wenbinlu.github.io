## What's found

We simulate a supernova in a close binary and show that the newborn neutron star can capture gas from its shock-inflated companion. The captured gas forms a disk. During a brief “propeller” phase, the rotating magnetosphere expels disk material and loses angular momentum, rapidly slowing the star.

Our models produce both ordinary pulsars and ultra-long-period neutron stars. The longest periods require strong initial magnetic fields, typically above $10^{14}$ G.

## Why it matters

This channel links binary stellar evolution to the unusual spins of ultra-long-period pulsars. It offers a formation mechanism for slowly rotating neutron stars, while the origin of their radio emission remains a separate question.
